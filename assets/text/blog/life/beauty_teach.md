Teaching board games – it's a necessary part of board games that I've found myself oddly fascinated with. I find myself volunteering to teach and even guide players that are unfamiliar with games. This extends even to board games that I don't enjoy and even ones that I’ve never played! 

I had once again found myself teaching the board game Canvas to a couple of strangers, that the oddness of this experience had finally dawned on me. Especially since I don't even particularly like Canvas, and I didn't even know the people I was teaching it to! Here I was, interacting with some total strangers and engaging in an intimate lecture on arbitrary rules that they agree to follow.

And yet somehow, this strange ritual allows us to break the barrier of mystery to form a human connection we all crave for deeply.

# The Logic of the Board Game
-----------------------------

To begin to grapple with this mystery, it will help to break down the fundamental logic of playing board games. Whether with strangers or close friends, the logic remains the same. A group of people agree to an arbitrary set of rules and agree to either compete or cooperate to achieve a goal that's laid out by these rules. At least for the moment, the players agree to enter into a shared life, where the game and its objectives are all that matters.

Central to this impromptu social contract is the “teach.” It's not exactly efficient to have everyone go over the rules individually, so one person should be the teacher introducing the logic and purpose that the game serves. It serves as a quasi-baptism into this shared world. 

The teacher is the guide, pointing at all of the possibilities in this world, while the “students” explore its boundaries. All culminating in the end of the game, as the class gets recapped and the post-game discussion ensues.

# The Value of a Shared Life
----------------------------

This temporary shared life is the fertile ground from which friendship can spring. Friendship is often defined as mutual benevolence with a shared life, and the board serves as a microcosm of that. This momentary frame allows us to act as if we're already friends.

While it might be a truism, it still bears repeating; man is a social animal. The board game serves as a social lubricant that eases and promotes connecting with the other. Moreover, the game allows us to experience the boundaries of our emotions in a place free of consequence. We see how we respond to defeat and to victory, to glory and to despair, without having to encounter the tragedies and struggle that often precedes these in real life.

This value proposition is inherently what you're offering whenever you invite someone to play a game with you. And this is only possible if we first learn how to initiate newbies into the game.

# The Importance of Humility
----------------------------

While the “teach” provider serves an integral role, he must not confuse his necessity with his own glory. I myself fell into this trap once upon a time. I felt that the people I thought didn't show the requisite gratitude and that I must punish their incredulity. Did they not see how gracious I was being for sharing this new game with them?

It's important for teachers to never ascribe the good of what they teach purely from themselves. In fact, the teach, while integral, is only a small part of the value of the game. There is also the game itself and its designers, and the active participation of the neophytes in the game. And while the teacher leads the relationship in this temporary shared life, he himself also benefits from it.

An overgrowth of pride can cloud the greater good of human connection with the insidious venom of self-conceit. Pride does precede destruction and my haughty spirit presaged a free fall of not just my gaming life but also of my life in general. And a humble spirit must be cultivated to reap the fruits of this shared life.

# The Friendship Mirror
-----------------------

The ultimate end of friendship is to assume a shared identity with the beloved. To be initiated in the same game serves as good fertile ground from which this communion can spring forth. This makes the role of teaching board games an important and necessary tool for achieving this end.

Cultivating this skill can serve as an excellent tool to achieve a shared life with other people, from your closest family to the most distant of strangers. And through this, we can more easily see ourselves reflected in other people. For our identity is only valuable once it comes into contact with community. For it is the interface by which we engage in it.

So play and teach your board games, and engage in this strange ritual. For through it, we can become more ourselves.
