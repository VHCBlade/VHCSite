As I watch my friend drive off, I consider that maybe starting school late has its perks. I still have a good half hour before the bus picks me up.

Oh well, I guess I’ll make the most of it.

As I pull out my binder, the soft breeze blows some leaves into my bag. I look up and notice a tree just outside the school. Has it always been there? Weird; maybe it’ll make good material for a poem.

I click my pen.

_As my vision breaks from falling leaves_

Hmmm… what rhymes with leaves? Sleeves? Seems difficult to make sense given the context. Ah, I know, “relieve.” Trees give shade which grants relief from the sun. It doesn’t exactly rhyme… but close enough. Now how am I gonna…

“Y’know you’re a loner Rian.”

I look up.

It is a girl. She is just there… suddenly standing next to me. She looks familiar, but she’s not in my grade. Being from a small school, it’s easy to remember everyone, but I feel like I know her from somewhere.

Also, what is with this accusatory tone! There’s nothing inherently wrong with being a loner. Wait a minute…

“I’m not a loner. I was literally just with my friend!”

“I find that hard to believe. Whenever I see you, your head is always in that notebook.”

“Binder.”

“What?”

“It’s not a notebook, it’s a binder.”

I am proud of this binder. It was given to me by my old English teacher. She put a note on the first page saying, “Keep Writing.” It inspires me every time I see it, though I wonder how she would feel if she knew that I filled half of the leaflets with math equations.

“Well whatever it is, you’re always writing in it. It wouldn’t kill you to speak to the people around you every now and then.”

She crossed her arms. Her voice was stern, but it didn’t seem angry– it sounded… familiar. But I just couldn’t place it. I look at her face. Her long hair was pulled back by a headband and fell over her shoulders. She offers a weak smile, but her eyes don’t agree. She seems… concerned?

I close my binder.

“The way I see it, other people are a gamble. Sometimes we’ll connect and it’ll be a ton of fun. Other times it’ll drag, and I’ll feel drained.” I lift my binder and show her the cover. “But when I’m in here, I know that I’ll have a good time.”

“That’s…” she pauses. 

She kicks a loose rock. It bounces towards the newfound tree. Its leaves continue to fall.

“Is that why you’re always in your own world?” She asks, “I sometimes wonder if we should check in on you. See if you’re okay.”

Check… something about her saying that sounds so familiar.

I let out a cheeky grin. “Y’know, if you wanted me to talk to you in Chess practice, you could have just asked.”

“That's not what… You’re so annoying.”

“You literally started this conversation by calling me a loner!”

We share a laugh. I guess I win this time.

She looks back at the binder. “What’s in that binder of yours anyway?”

I grip it tightly. I’ve actually never shown it to anyone… but maybe it’s OK. There’s a first time for everything.

“Here,” I say, handing her the binder, “why don’t you see for yourself.”

“Are these… MATH EQUATIONS? You really are a loner.”

“Not that.” I flip to the page I’d been working on. “Here.”

She read it silently. “Are you writing about that tree?”

“Yeah, I’ve actually never noticed it before. It just kinda jumped out at me.”

The bus arrives. She hands me back the binder as I sling my bag over my shoulder.

I start telling her. “Maybe you’re right. Maybe I should put this ‘notebook’ away sometimes.”

“Yeah, of course I’m right. You should definitely listen to me, Rian.” She pauses. “Alright, see you tomorrow?”

She really is annoying. 

But maybe that’s a good thing.

“Yeah,” I said “I guess I will Sika.”

The bus pulls away. I turn to look back at the window. 

Sika is still standing there, looking up at that tree– its leaves still falling.
