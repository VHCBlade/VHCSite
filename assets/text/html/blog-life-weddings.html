Weddings

In my adult life, I've attended three weddings: two for my highschool friends and one for my beloved brother.

Compared to older generations, this seems few. My parents had gotten married when my mother was only 23–a common age for everyone in their generation. I wonder if it's because this generation doesn't value marriage in the same way. (Or maybe getting locked down for two years has something to do with it)

This is a shame. Marriage is a natural calling that leads people to the most meaningful relationship of their lives. Most people have existential crises that would easily be solved by simply getting married. "What is my life for?" "Oh it's that wedding ring." And the children that often follow it are even better (ask a parent; they'll tell you).

However, I think there's a more… urgent reason that this rarity is a shame.

Weddings are Awesome.

The Guaranteed Reunion

As we go through life, our younger friendships tend to drift. We see our old friends less, and we start having other priorities.

If you're young right now, you might disagree, and think that your friend group is the exception.

It will happen to you too.

You're not special.

You might remain close to one or two friends, but even then, friendship will be much less important in your life than it once was. While this is sad, it's natural. And as you get busy, you might not find the time to meet with your friends anymore.

That is until one of you gets married. All of a sudden, you'll make the time to attend their wedding. After all, what else are you going to do? And guess who you're going to meet there? All of your old friends!

I've thoroughly enjoyed talking with my friends at weddings. They remind me of a time when life was simpler and talking was easier. And we get to do it with the backdrop of one of our own becoming a man.

Friendship is one of the things that gives life meaning. The couple– sharing their bond through a wedding– rekindles that fire and brings it to the forefront. The love of the couple overflows and waters the forgotten fields of old friendship.

Weddings are always fun to attend. You'll be filled with love for your old friends.

The Entertaining Speeches

And what would a wedding be without the speeches? For many people, this will be the only time they give a public address. And what better address than to honor someone that they love. The gravity of this speech can make a master speaker of even the most reclused, and the love on display can make even the most stone-hearted cry.

The parents usually give better speeches than the best man or the maid of honor, but hey, it's to be expected, they have a lot more experience. (And are a lot less full of themselves)

To prepare for the speech, the speaker needs to distill the relationship they have with the newlywed couple. They really have to find the anchor moments that really get to who their beloved is. It's a careful act of love to send the groom or bride on their way.

And this love overflows. It rubs off on all who listen. I always leave weddings feeling high on life, with a renewed hope in humanity. Love drips from every word from the mouth of the speaker, lighting the room with its meaning.

Furthermore, as someone who loves writing, the speeches inspire me to write even more. Speeches are great, especially when they're about something as meaningful as friendship or fatherhood.

Give wedding speeches, behind the stand, Honor lives and bonds, now stitched and grand.

The Culmination of a Life

Weddings are grand because they represent the culmination of the life of the couple. They die to their individuality and step into a new life. One that they will live with each other and for each other.

When you're single, it's easy to feel lost and restless. What should I do? Where do I go? Who should I be? Sometimes you'll be so tense that you just want to scream! "God, what am I supposed to do with my life? What is my life for?"

But once you're married, it all becomes clear. Your life is for your wife, and your wife's life is for you. You no longer have to ask what you should do. You just need to look down at your fingers, and clasp it in your wife's hands. A ring representing the union you now have.

All of this is layered into the wedding vows. The couple's covenant, now sealed by God, is what we bear witness to. And as human beings, we are naturally drawn to the magnitude of this event. It's why we have graduations, birthday parties, and anniversaries.

Moreover, the gravity of this moment makes it all the more enjoyable. We are infected by the meaning of the moment for the couple. We too become happier because they are stepping into a new and important stage in their lives. It's a celebration that is like no other– literally once in a lifetime.

The wedding marks the end of two Now beat as one, a single tune

Cheers to Weddings

Weddings are truly great. Before I attended my first wedding, I didn't really understand what all the hype was about. To me, it just seemed like a giant waste of money.

However, as I stood at Mass seeing my high school friend get married, I understood. The wedding isn't just an arbitrary show of prosperity– though some may misuse a wedding for that end. It's actually a celebration of the union of two lives. That feeling was further sealed when I attended my brother's wedding. (The priest giving an actual life-changing homily probably helped. But that's a story for another time.)

I look forward to when my next friend will get married. There really isn't anything better in this stage of my life.

A celebration where love abounds The sweetest day, with ringing sounds

So sing the praise of the wedding day!
