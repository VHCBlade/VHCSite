Complaining About Complaining

I am a top-tier complainer.

Just ask my brother. Day in and day out, especially when we were in college, he'd often counsel me by simply saying that I complain too much. I should simply accept things.

And my favorite thing to complain about? Other people's complaints.

I'd complain about how ungrateful people were.

I'd complain that they were never satisfied.

I'd complain about hearing their complaints over and over again.

My brother would always point out that I was also complaining. And really, I was guilty of all of the same faults I was complaining about.

"That's different!" I'd protest.

But was it really? At the time, I would try to convince myself that what I was doing was different. I wasn't just complaining… I was… critiquing. I just wanted them to realize that their complaints were misplaced. I was helping.

What I've realized, however, is that there's no getting around it.

All complaining is bad for you.

A Pessimistic Outlook

Complaints are often borne out of a truth:

The traffic was bad;

The weather was awful;

The server messed up your order.

Objectively, it might be true. Maybe there was a car crash today that slowed down traffic, or a storm was passing through. Surely there's nothing wrong with just stating the truth?

Complaining , however, is more than just truth telling. Complaints always come with a judgement. Rather than "The traffic is bad," it's "The city is poorly managed." Not "The server made a mistake" but "People these days can't even do a simple job."

Acknowledging something negative doesn't have to come with a judgement about the nature of the world– yet complaining causes us to judge anyway.

When complaining becomes a pastime, it fills the air with a pessimistic miasma. Our minds become accustomed to jumping to the worst conclusion. And when we jump to the worst, we begin to expect it.

If everything is always bad and outside of your control, what reason would you have to change anything? If things are going to fail anyway, then why ever try?

Furthermore, we begin to even detest the good. When good things happen, they frustrate our complaints. So we rejoice when bad things happen and attempt to justify our complaints when good things happen. Worse still, maybe we'll just look for something else to complain about.

We begin to hate what is good, and love what is bad.

Is that really the kind of world you want to live in?

A Furious Ingrate

Complaining also frustrates gratitude– even about things unrelated to the object of complaint. What you'll find is that it's difficult to feel grateful and want to complain at the same time.

Complaints breed ingratitude.

Have you ever done a favor for someone who then complained about how you did it?

Maybe it's a friend who borrowed your car and then complained about it needing gas.

Or someone you helped move who then complained about how long it was taking.

Does it make you happy? Does it make you want to help them again?

Of course not!

Ingrates drain all of the joy out of helping them. Instead of feeling good about going out of your way to do something nice, you instead resent that you even bothered to try. How could they be so callous? Can they not see that you're trying your best? Would it hurt to show just a little bit of gratitude?

And yet, when it comes to our own complaints, we are so quick to excuse our own ingratitude. We instead focus on what we "deserve."

I deserve a more understanding boss.

I deserve a more generous government.

I deserve a friend that will look over my faults.

That last one is particularly ironic.

What exactly is it that makes you deserve these things? Are you perfect? Clearly not– otherwise you wouldn't have faults to overlook.

Complaints often breed a posture of "deserving." And when we don't get what we deserve, we become angry. We lash out and complain that the world isn't exactly as we hope it would be.

However, there is a rather unpopular truth. You don't deserve anything. Everything you have is a gift. You live in a world that has gone to great lengths to prevent you from suffering. Most of us had parents who raised us and loved us. Even our very life is a blessing.

What can you give to repay that?

Instead of complaining, we should develop a posture of gratitude. We should be grateful for all of the blessings in our lives. And when we become grateful, complaining becomes difficult.

A Haughty Spirit

The last reason that complaining is corrosive is that it breeds that oh-so-deadly sin of pride.

When you cast judgement by complaining, you are looking down. It's easy to confuse knowing how something can be better with actually being better.

And when you spend all of your time looking down, you'll forget that there are always higher things.

My own complaining about complaining was especially destructive. The clear hypocrisy was easily excused in my mind. I was simply trying to cultivate a spirit of virtue and humility.

I would think to myself, "Things would be so much better if they were just like me. Humble."

The truth is, all complaining is vicious.

And when you cultivate a haughty spirit, it spoils your other virtues as well.

Why not lie? After all, I know better.

Why not bribe? It's nothing compared to the corrupt politicians.

Why help my friends? They're the ones that need me more. They should do things for me.

Is this someone who should be emulated?

Instead of complaining, we should instead focus on cultivating virtue within our own lives. Because we all are sinners and fall short of the glory of God.

Never Complain

The truth is, nothing good ever comes from complaining.

Whether it's small,

Or earned,

Or "righteous."

Complaining will always create a miasma that ruins your life. It makes you dread the future, angry and ungrateful, and deify yourself.

So let the traffic just be slow.

Let the weather just be bad.

Let the waiter mess up your order.

And let it be nothing more.

After all these years I see now that my brother was right. It's better to just accept things and let them be. It is not my job to judge everything I come across.

You should take great effort to avoid complaining at all costs. Especially when you're tempted to complain about complaining.
